# M9 Invalid, not failed

## Goal

Make a verdict trustworthy (G3): no environment failure is ever reported as the mod's failure, and when a run goes wrong a person or an AI agent can find out why from the running game, without a restart.

## Demo

During a run on a dedicated session with two clients, `p2`'s process is suspended, and in a second session ended: each time the check is `invalid` naming `p2`, the checks after it are `session-broken`, `inspect` returns `p2` `lost` within 20 s, and the result file holds `p2`'s log up to its end and any crash dump.

## Stories

- `US-010` (system fail) A reset that cannot complete makes the next check invalid
- `US-015` (system fail) A machine stops answering during a check
- `US-016` (system fail) A game process crashes during a check
- `US-041` (feature) Every check ends pass, fail or invalid with its reason
- `US-043` (system fail) The logs of a machine that crashed are kept
- `US-044` (feature) Ask a running game what is going on
- `US-045` (system fail) Inspecting a machine that stopped answering
- `US-059` (system fail) A machine's game clock stops while it still answers

## What it builds

- The runner ceiling on every order and check (`OBS-03`), a stall (`P-07`) and a crash with its dumps (`P-09`), the order of causes (`DM-06`) and their times in the result (`RES-05`).
- The heartbeat and clock watch (`HLT-02`) and the health fields of `INS-04`.
- The reset's ceiling and a reset that fails (`RST-04`).
- `inspect`, `exec`, `logs` and `errors` on a `broken` session and on a `lost` machine (`INS-01`, `INS-03`).
- The fixture's extension `skdfx_x64.dll`, `skdfx_fnc_crash`, `skdfx_fnc_exit` and `skdfx_fnc_holdClock` (`FIX-01`, `FIX-02`).
- `docs/`: the outcomes and their reasons, and diagnosing a broken session.

## Spikes

- **`SPK-16`**, whole, runs before the work that depends on it; its pass condition is in `spec/06-open-questions.md`. If it fails: The fixture's crash ends the process with `ExitProcess` and an access violation's exit code, and the dumps of `US-016` and `US-043` are tested with a dump the harness writes of the live process. The fixture contract's `skdfx_fnc_crash` is edited to say so; what the stories promise holds.
- **`SPK-17`**, whole, runs before the work that depends on it; its pass condition is in `spec/06-open-questions.md`. If it fails: `FIX-02` is dropped from the fixture contract, the clock-watch rule is proven by the runner's own tests without the game, and `US-059` is accepted by the owner cutting the Shadow stream during a check.
- **`SPK-15`**, whole, runs before the work that depends on it; its pass condition is in `spec/06-open-questions.md`. If it fails: The clock watch reports but does not change outcomes until thresholds hold. That changes the promise: a stopped clock flags the machine in `status` but no check ends `unhealthy`, so `US-059`'s first and third criteria wait, and `HLT-02` and `US-059` are edited to say so.

## Exit criteria

1. `US-010`, `US-015`, `US-016`, `US-041`, `US-043`, `US-044`, `US-045` and `US-059` pass their acceptance criteria on the test machine.
2. Over the milestone's acceptance runs, every check ended by a lost machine, a broken session, a failed reset or reload, or a stopped run is `invalid` (`NFR-RL-01`).
3. The spikes' results are in `research/`, and the spec is edited in place to what they found.
4. Every check and test it adds carries the story or requirement ID it proves (`NFR-MT-02`), the gates of `NFR-MT-03` pass, and `docs/` say how to use what it built.
5. The owner has run the demo on the Windows test machine.

## Left out

The rest of the health watch, `HLT-01` (a `should`).
